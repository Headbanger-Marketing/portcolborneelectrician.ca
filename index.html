Before work begins

Give each item a reason to repair, replace or stay.

Consider a property where equipment meets moisture or weather: an exterior receptacle, a porch light, an outlet in the laundry room. Each of them may need a different decision, and some may stay just as they are.

Photograph each fitting from the outside, note any cracked cover, rust, staining or discolouration, and write down why you think it needs work. Bring those notes so the contractor can say what to repair, replace or leave.

How do I know if something should be repaired or replaced?

A contractor should explain the finding, not just the age of the item. Ask what was observed, what repair would involve, what replacement would involve, and what leaving it alone would mean, all in writing.

Can a cracked outdoor cover be swapped without wider work?

Sometimes a cover is only a cover, but the contractor should look at what sits behind it. Ask whether moisture has reached the device or the box, and whether the fitting is rated for the location.

What should a written quote say about parts left in place?

It should list each item that will be repaired, each that will be replaced and each that will stay, with a short reason beside it. Compare quotes line by line on that basis, not on the total.

Who decides whether an older fitting can stay in use?

The Licensed Electrical Contractor assesses the fitting and gives a recommendation, and the property owner decides. Where a rented or shared space is involved, the landlord or condominium corporation may need to approve the choice first.

After the form is sent, who stands behind the visit and the work?

Any provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor you deal with stands behind its visit and work. It must confirm separately who it is and which licence it holds; check it with ESA first.
