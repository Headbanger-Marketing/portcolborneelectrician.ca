Electrical decisions belong early in the renovation

A renovation changes how the space will be used and what equipment it must serve. Provide the drawings and appliance specifications before the electrical scope is finalized. A Licensed Electrical Contractor can assess retained wiring, new circuits and the proposed loads. Municipal building permissions and ESA electrical notification are separate matters, and the project may require both.

Prepare the scope for your Port Colborne property

Sketch the new room and the old exterior wall it joins, and mark every existing receptacle, switch, light and exterior fitting that the work will touch or cover. Photograph them before anything is closed in.

Ask which existing circuits and exterior fittings will be kept, which will be replaced and which will be removed or covered over. Ask whether any porch fitting must change because it will now be indoors.

What should the assessment establish?

Going ahead with the wiring for an addition or enclosed porch, and choosing when, rests with the owner. The Port Colborne job's scope names the existing wiring staying in use and whether its condition suits the new space. New circuits, receptacle positions and lighting are listed against the drawings, with who handles the ESA notification and whether a building permit is also needed. A porch fitting made for an exposed spot may need replacing once enclosed, or may be fine to leave; the scope records which. Concealed or covered wiring is noted before walls close, with any reason to keep it.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can the wiring in the old exterior wall stay once the porch is enclosed?

Possibly, but the contractor must assess it first, since a wall that was outside may now be inside and hold exterior fittings. Ask for a written note of what stays, what is replaced and why.

At what stage of an addition should electrical planning start?

Early, before walls are framed and closed. Give the contractor the drawings and appliance details so the scope can list circuits, exterior fittings and any existing items that will be reused or left.

Prepare your enquiry

Useful project details

  • Drawings and intended use of each space
  • Appliance and equipment model information
  • Construction schedule and other trades
  • Existing installation records if available
  • Person authorized to approve changes

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
