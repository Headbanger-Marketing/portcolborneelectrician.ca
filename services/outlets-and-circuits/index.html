An extra receptacle does not establish extra capacity

Receptacle locations and circuit design answer different questions. More convenient plug-in points may share the same existing circuit. New appliances or specialty equipment may need a different connection, a dedicated circuit or other assessed work. Give the Licensed Electrical Contractor the equipment information and intended location so the proposal follows the actual use.

Prepare the scope for your Port Colborne property

Find the rating label and installation sheet for the appliance, and write down whether it sits in a damp basement, a laundry room or an exposed outdoor spot. Photograph any existing receptacle or disconnect near that place.

Ask whether the present receptacle or circuit can keep serving this appliance, needs replacing or is better left for other loads. Ask who makes the connection and who files the notification.

What should the assessment establish?

A heating or cooling appliance is being added to a Port Colborne home. The contractor looks at how it will be connected, where it will sit and how exposed that spot is to moisture or weather. Every other load already on the same circuit is listed so that none is overlooked. Whether the present wiring can serve the appliance, must be replaced or is better left to its current use is a finding the record states plainly. Written up, the scope gives what is new, what is kept and why, and names any disconnect or cover the location calls for.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Does a new heating or cooling appliance always need its own circuit?

Not always, and the contractor assesses it after reviewing the appliance sheet and the existing wiring. Never plug it into a nearby outlet to find out. Request the contractor's reasoning in written form.

Can I leave an old receptacle in place beside the new appliance?

It might stay if its condition and location suit it, though nobody should assume it can feed the new appliance. Have the contractor name which outlets remain and which are swapped out.
